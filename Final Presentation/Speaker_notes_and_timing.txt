Motivation
• Grinding needs the tool face aligned with the physical surface. The configured normal comes from calibration against the plate, with residual uncertainty.
• With a tilted tool, normal pressing creates a contact moment. Low rotational stiffness lets this moment turn the tool towards alignment.
• Cartesian impedance makes position and rotation compliant. The desired orientation stays fixed while the rotational spring and damper remain active.

Cartesian pose
• The end effector (EE) is the tool or gripper at the end of the arm. Its Cartesian pose describes position and rotation relative to the robot base frame.
• Position uses x, y, and z, while roll, pitch, and yaw illustrate rotation. Together they give six degrees of freedom.
• Forward kinematics calculates the pose from all seven joint angles, with p=p(q) for position. The controller uses a rotation matrix for orientation.

Cartesian impedance controller
• Positional error points from measured to desired position. Rotational error uses a correction angle φ and unit axis u.
• The sketch compares measured and desired tool orientations at one position. The axis points out of the page.
• Stiffness produces force and moment. Damping opposes velocity differences.
• Force and moment form a wrench. Its six-by-six stiffness and damping matrices have three-by-three blocks, with zero off-diagonal blocks here.
• Before the point shift, the stiffness and damping matrices are diagonal in the surface frame.

Real-time control
• The robot receives joint torques. We generate the Cartesian contribution from the wrench using the Jacobian transpose:
• The Jacobian maps the seven joint velocities to the EE's linear and angular velocities. Model compensation and enabled null-space torques complete the command.
• The measured pose feeds back into the control loop. The C++ controller uses libfranka and updates every millisecond.

Translation–rotation coupling
• The tool centre point (TCP) is the controlled reference point attached to the EE. The centre of compliance (CoC) at p_c is a virtual reference for stiffness and damping.
• Moving the CoC changes how position and rotation errors generate force and moment. Its displacement from the TCP is r_c = p_c - p_TCP.
• The A-transpose and A transformations shift both stiffness and damping to the TCP. The resulting matrices act on the TCP pose error and velocity differences.
• Their off-diagonal blocks couple translation and rotation. This uses the local small-rotation model.

Contact experiment
• The process goes from tool orientation and surface approach to contact establishment, then grinding. These experiments evaluate the five-second contact-establishment phase.
• Low normal stiffness allows compliant pressing, while higher tangential stiffness resists sideways movement. Rotational stiffness is higher about the normal and lower about the surface tangents.
• The position reference advances into the surface to generate pressing force. The desired orientation stays at its entry value, so contact moments drive the rotation.

Null-space controller
• The contact results are complete. The separate null-space study concerns the arm's redundant joint motion. At full Jacobian rank, seven joints leave one null-space direction with zero instantaneous EE motion.
• Damping reduces null-space motion by opposing redundant joint velocity.
• Conditioning chooses between two local null-space directions and drives towards the one with the larger minimum singular value. It can initiate joint motion. At a singularity, the EE loses an instantaneous motion direction. Near it, some EE motions require very large joint velocities. A larger minimum singular value indicates better local conditioning under the same Jacobian convention.

Jacobian conditioning
• A smaller minimum singular value means some tool motions require larger joint velocities. Zero means the EE loses an instantaneous motion direction.
• It falls with no null-space torque and with damping. Both conditioning settings keep it nearly constant during the disturbance.
• Curves show three-trial means over the same four-second interval and use the same Jacobian convention. Shading shows one sample standard deviation.

Net joint motion
• Net joint motion measures projected joint movement remaining after opposite motions cancel. It is 7.52° without null-space torque and 5.61° with damping.
• Mean net joint motion values with conditioning are only 1.5×10^-2 ° and -1.1×10^-2 °. Conditioning counters the disturbance in the tested direction.
• Cumulative joint motion values are 0.29° and 1.69°. The larger value is consistent with more back-and-forth motion despite similarly small net joint motion.

Conclusion
• Contact reduced angular error for both larger entry tilts while the desired orientation stayed fixed. The error is relative to the calibrated surface and retains calibration and tool-mount uncertainty.
• Higher rotational stiffness increased angular error from 1.75° to 6.58°.
• CoC displacement changes angular error and can carry it through zero. At the TCP, the added moment vanishes while the rotational spring and damper remain active.
• Damping reduced arm motion, and conditioning countered the tested disturbance direction. Nearly zero net joint motion coexisted with back-and-forth motion.
• Future work is a more rigid tool mount and adaptive CoC selection. After alignment, the adaptive CoC should return to the TCP.

Null-space torques and projector
• The kinematic projector selects null-space torque directions. It uses the Jacobian \mboxpseudoinverse J^+.
• Damping and conditioning add to give the total null-space torque.
• Damping opposes projected joint velocity and is zero at rest. d_null is the damping coefficient.
• Conditioning probes both directions along the unit null-space vector v_7. It chooses the direction with the larger minimum singular value.
• The selector s_σ is +1 or -1, and zero within the deadband. k_σ sets the torque magnitude, which can be nonzero at rest.
